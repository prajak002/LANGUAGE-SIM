# Exploratory check: Chatterji 1926 (2026-10-11)

**Not part of the frozen study.** Done after all Wiktionary labels were unblinded, with no frozen rule, by text search of an OCR copy. It produces no labels and changes no count in `docs/feasibility_report.md`.

- **Source:** S. K. Chatterji, *The Origin and Development of the Bengali Language* (1926), Internet Archive item `OriginDevelopmentOfBengali` (marked Public Domain Mark on the item page). OCR text read in a temporary folder; nothing stored here but page references and paraphrases.
- **Scope:** the 6 Bengali senses whose Wiktionary candidate is semi-learned.
- **Result:** 2 are explicitly called semi-tatsama (`sts.`); 2 appear in a list that opens with that label, where the OCR does not show how far the label extends; 2 were not found.
- **Also verified from this source (vol. 1, Appendix D, §111, pp. 189–191):** the definitions of tatsama, tadbhava and semi-tatsama used in the paper. Chatterji notes that "tatsama" in modern use refers to a word's form, not to how it entered the language, and attributes the term semi-tatsama to Grierson and other European scholars.
- **Limits:** page numbers come from OCR running heads and may be off by one. The Bengali script and diacritics are garbled in the OCR. **A person must check each passage on the page images before the paper relies on it.**
- Searches for the other Bengali words were run at the same time and are not reported as results.
